## 2. Supabase production - Disk IO Budget warning, and what it turned up

Supabase emailed on Sep 12 saying production (mtfyekdxtkdiaqbgaoza) was
depleting its Disk IO Budget. Chasing that found two real faults that had been
running since spring.

### Fault 1: TWO reminder cron jobs, both firing every 5 minutes

`cron.job` held both:

- **jobid 3**, `send-reminders-every-5min`
- **jobid 7**, `send-reminders-5min`

So `send-reminders` was called **576 times a day instead of 288**. No duplicate
texts ever went out, purely because `reminder_sends` has a UNIQUE constraint on
(user_id, slot, local_date). The idempotency table hid the problem for months.

**Job 3 had never worked at all.** Every one of its calls failed instantly with
"A libcurl function was given a bad argument" - it posted with no body. Job 3
was unscheduled on Sep 12.

### Fault 2: the surviving job was timing out on every call

`net._http_response` showed job 7 hitting pg_net's default 5000 ms timeout on
nearly every tick, with only a handful of 200s. Reminders still went out - the
edge function keeps running after Postgres stops listening - but there was no
visibility into success or failure, and each call held a connection for a full
five seconds.

Job 7 was rescheduled with `timeout_milliseconds := 30000` and with BOTH an
`apikey` and an `Authorization: Bearer` header.

**Verified afterward: six consecutive scheduled ticks returned 200, none timed
out.** That is the first hard proof the reminders path works end to end.

### Fault 3: cron history had never been pruned

`cron.job_run_details` held **49,440 rows** going back to 2026-05-17. Cleared to
the last 7 days, and a nightly purge job added:

- **jobid 8**, `purge-cron-history`, `0 4 * * *`

`net._http_response` does NOT need this - pg_net expires its own rows after
about six hours.

### What the IO warning actually is

Two charts settled it:

- **Memory:** swap is in use constantly. Memory commitment is 1.25 GB against
  roughly 950 MB of RAM on the Free tier instance.
- **Cache hit ratio: 100.00%** - 213 million cache hits against 2,017 disk
  reads. Postgres is essentially never reading from disk.

So the drain is **write** IO, not reads: WAL, checkpoints, autovacuum, and the
constant small writes the cron machinery generated. Halving the cron work should
show up.

**Decision: do not buy compute on a guess.** Wait a few days and see whether
Supabase warns again. If it goes quiet, the cron cleanup was the fix. The Pro
upgrade still belongs before launch regardless, for the daily backups -
production currently has none.

### Security follow-up

The cron job command carries an `sb_secret_...` service key inline, and that key
was pasted into a chat transcript on Sep 12. **Rotate it before launch**, and
remember the cron job command has to be updated with the new key at the same
time, or reminders start returning 401.

Watch for that specific failure: a wrong or placeholder key produces an instant
401, not a timeout.

## 4. Launch sequence - questions settled this week

- **There is no partial release for a 1.0.** Once Release This Version is
  pressed, the app is public in the US. Phased release, which trickles a version
  out over seven days, is only available for updates to an app that is already
  live.
- **Approval and release are separate, and that is the lever.** The version can
  sit at Pending Developer Release indefinitely while the wipe happens and the
  five testers sign up fresh through TestFlight against the clean database. All
  of that is private.
- **After release, existing accounts persist.** Accounts live in Supabase, not
  on the phone. Installing from the App Store over the TestFlight build keeps
  the session; deleting first means signing back in with phone and SMS code, not
  signing up again. Deleting and reinstalling is the better test, since it
  proves you are on the store build.
- **One thing TestFlight cannot test:** the referral chain. `referred_by` is set
  only at signup, so the QR to Branch to tree test needs a device that has never
  had the app and a phone number with no account. That is the Part D test.
- **TestFlight builds expire 90 days after upload.** Days or a couple of weeks
  at Pending Developer Release is fine; months is not.

## 5. PayPal rates for nonprofits, for reference

| Route | Fee |
|---|---|
| PayPal Giving Fund (what the app uses now) | 0% |
| Confirmed charity rate, paid direct | 1.99% + $0.49 |
| Standard business rate | 2.89% + $0.49 |

International adds roughly 1.5%. Qualifying for the 1.99% rate needs the EIN, a
bank statement and the IRS determination letter, all of which exist; review
takes about three business days.

The trade is donor data. Giving Fund takes nothing but routes through PayPal's
own 501(c)(3), pays out on a delay, and hands over no donor names or emails. On
a $25 gift the charity rate costs about 99 cents. Worth revisiting once real
donations exist and thanking donors starts to matter. Confirm the numbers on
PayPal's own fee page before acting.
