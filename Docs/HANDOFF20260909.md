## 1. App Store rejection - Guideline 3.1.1 (the day's main event)

### What happened

Apple rejected iOS 1.0, build 1.0.0 (97) on the morning of Sep 9. The email
Apple sends contains no reason; the detail lives in App Store Connect under
App Review -> Resolution Center.

There have been two rejections on this submission, not one:

- **Sep 3, Guideline 2.1** - a standard information request for a developer
  account with limited review history. They wanted a screen recording, a
  description of purpose and audience, setup instructions, and a list of
  external services. Gary replied the same night. That item cleared.
- **Sep 9, Guideline 3.1.1** - Business: Payments - In-App Purchase. This is
  the live blocker.

Apple's words:

> "We noticed that the app allows users to contribute donations to the
> development of the app with a mechanism other than In-App Purchase."

Reviewed on an iPad Air 11-inch (M3). Submission ID
fa58d07e-1aae-436c-afc5-9404ecab1bba. Apple attached a screenshot of the
screen that triggered it.

### Why this is a misreading, and the rule that actually applies

Guideline 3.2.2(iv) covers this case directly:

> "Unless you are an approved nonprofit or otherwise permitted under Section
> 3.2.1 (vi) above, collecting funds within the app for charities and
> fundraisers. Apps that seek to raise money for such causes must be free on
> the App Store and may only collect funds outside of the app, such as via
> Safari or SMS."

That is exactly the implementation:

- The app is free and every feature is available to every user. Donating
  unlocks nothing.
- Donations go to 30 Acts of Kindness NFP, a 501(c)(3) public charity,
  EIN 41-4058016.
- No payment is taken in-app. PayPal opens the Giving Fund charity page in
  Safari; Zelle copies the address to the clipboard for the donor's own bank
  app; Venmo is hidden.

### The actual cause

Found in `src/screens/DonationScreen.js`. The hero subtitle read:

> "Your donation helps us spread kindness worldwide **and keep this app free
> for everyone**."

That final clause is what the reviewer read as funding app development. The
rest of the screen was already compliant - the 501(c)(3) name and EIN were
displayed, and every payment route led outside the app.

This was a one-sentence copy problem, not an architecture problem.

### What was done

**Replied in Resolution Center** (sent Sep 9) explaining the nonprofit status,
citing 3.2.2(iv), confirming that nothing is unlocked by donating, and
committing to remove the ambiguous phrase in the next build. The IRS
determination letter (Letter 947, issued May 12 2026) was attached
proactively rather than waiting to be asked.

Reply first, appeal later if needed - the App Review Board appeal link is in
the same dialog, and appeals go better with a plain explanation already on
the record.

## 3. Apple nonprofit approval - started today

Worth doing because it is slow, not because it affects the pending review.

### The path is Candid, not Benevity

Benevity is for organizations outside the US. For a US nonprofit, Apple
approves anyone holding a **Candid Seal of Transparency**, and the seal is
free. Every seal holder is automatically approved to accept donations through
Apple Pay.

### Progress

- Confirmed the organization is already in Candid's database as
  "30 Acts of Kindness NFP", Chicago IL, EIN 41-4058016, ruling year 2026,
  NTEE code S20. No IRS propagation delay, which was the main risk.
- Candid profile: app.candid.org/profile/16619778
- Registered a Candid account and started the profile claim.
- **Snag:** Candid explicitly refuses the IRS determination letter. It accepts
  only an IRS Affirmation Letter or the EIN Issuance Letter (CP 575).
- Gary had the CP575E (issued February 4, 2026). The Bizee cover page was
  stripped and the IRS page alone was submitted as a clean single-page PDF.
- Sent to Candid for verification. Awaiting profile administrator access.

Fallback if the CP575E is ever rejected: call the IRS TE/GE line at
877-829-5500 and request an affirmation letter for EIN 41-4058016.

### What approval does and does not buy

Approval permits collecting donations **inside** the app, but guideline
3.2.1(vi) then requires offering Apple Pay, which means a supported payment
provider such as Stripe. Apple takes no commission on donations; the
processor will. The current PayPal Giving Fund route is fee-free.

So: take the free seal now, and treat the Apple Pay implementation as a
separate decision later, once the processing cost is known.

### Next step when access arrives

Fill the profile to Bronze seal level - mission statement, contact
information, website, leadership. No financials required, which suits an
organization with no 990 yet. Suggested areas of interest: community
improvement (already assigned), volunteerism promotion, and civic
participation or social connection if offered. Two or three areas is right;
avoid claiming mental health or youth development.

gguttman-ui expires in one day. Nothing in the project depends on it as far as
can be determined; the name suggests it was created for earlier SMS work.

Recommendation: let it expire. If something breaks, regenerating takes a
minute, and an unused long-lived token is standing risk.

rather than using the link in the email. That "your token is expiring" message
is a common phishing template. This one is probably genuine, but the direct
route costs nothing.
