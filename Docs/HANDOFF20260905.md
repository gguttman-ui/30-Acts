## THE REVIEWER ACCOUNT - WHAT HAPPENED, AND ITS STATE NOW

Worth reading before touching that account again.

The review account 5550100100 had a single act, so the calendar looked empty and
the certificate was unreachable. We seeded 29 days so day 30 could be completed
on camera.

Three things went wrong, in order:

1. The first seed used Postgres CURRENT_DATE, which is UTC. At 9 PM Chicago that
   is already tomorrow, so every row landed a day late. Fixed by shifting the
   rows, which then collided with the unique index on (user_phone, local_date)
   because Postgres checks that constraint row by row. Sidestepped by moving the
   rows out 1000 days and back 1001.
2. During the first recording, DELETE MY ACCOUNT was run while signed in as
   5550100100 - the reviewer account - rather than 5550100142. It worked
   exactly as designed: the login was removed and all 30 acts were anonymised to
   the phone DELETED-0e9a1eb2e237 with their stories stripped. Signing back in
   created a fresh, empty account.
3. Rebuilt with Desktop\rebuild-reviewer-account.sql, this time anchored to
   Chicago time. Then re-recorded.

STATE NOW: 5550100100 has a completed 30-day run (day 30 was logged during the
second recording), a certificate, and a recognition_orders row. 5550100142
exists and is blank - it was deleted on camera and signed back in afterwards.

IF THE ACCOUNT EVER NEEDS RESETTING: rebuild-reviewer-account.sql on the Desktop
does the whole job and is safe to run twice. It clears the old rows, restores the
profile's phone and name, deletes the recognition order so the day-30 flow
replays, and seeds 29 days ending yesterday.

## ACCOUNT DELETION - VERIFIED WORKING, WITH TWO GAPS

We tested delete_my_account() properly on Thursday night.

IT WORKS. The auth login is deleted, the profile row is deleted, and every
completion is anonymised: user_phone becomes DELETED-<hash>, and user_email,
notes and recipient are cleared. Confirmed on a real account, and 30 rows in the
database carry a DELETED- phone from earlier deletions.

The one failure we saw was specific: an old hand-made test account whose
profiles.phone was null. The function matches completions on that phone, so a
null there means the acts survive untouched. Accounts created through the app
always have a phone, so real users are fine.

TWO GAPS, both logged, neither a submission blocker, NEITHER TO BE TOUCHED WHILE
THE APP IS IN REVIEW:

- recognition_orders is never cleared. It holds ship_name, ship_street1,
  ship_city and ship_zip - a home address. Someone who orders a bracelet and
  later deletes their account leaves their address behind. The review notes tell
  Apple that deletion removes the account and its content.
- act_title survives. notes is cleared but the title is not, and a custom act
  title is something the user wrote.

Close both before there are real users with real addresses.

The 601 are mechanically clean: no malformed addresses, no duplicate addresses.
Three things worth doing before you send, all of which I can do on request:

1. 24 PEOPLE APPEAR TWICE - the multi-address contacts. Send to the whole file
   and 24 people get it twice. Collapse to one address each.
2. ABOUT 19 CORPORATE ADDRESSES - jpmorgan.com (9), promontory.com (10). Old
   colleagues at old jobs. Most will bounce, and bounces are what get a sender
   flagged.
3. 168 ADDRESSES ON LEGACY CONSUMER DOMAINS - aol.com (63), yahoo.com (57),
   hotmail.com (27), earthlink.net, optonline.net, nyc.rr.com. Many are live,
   but they are the oldest addresses and AOL and Yahoo are the strictest about
   unfamiliar senders. Put them in a later batch, not the first.

SENDING: Gmail caps a personal account near 500 recipients a day, and one send
to hundreds of BCC addresses is the classic spam signature. Batches of 50 to 75
over a fortnight. Your own address in To, everyone else in BCC - never To or CC,
or you publish everyone's address to everyone else.
